### Technical Charter (the "Charter")

for

### React a Series of LF Projects, LLC

*Adopted July 24, 2026*

</div>

This Charter sets forth the responsibilities and procedures for technical contribution to, and oversight of, the React open source project, which has been established as React a Series of LF Projects, LLC (the "Project"). LF Projects, LLC ("LF Projects") is a Delaware series limited liability company. All contributors (including committers, maintainers, and other technical positions) and other participants in the Project (collectively, "Collaborators") must comply with the terms of this Charter.

### 1. Mission and Scope of the Project

a. The mission of the Project is to develop and maintain React and associated open source software for building user interfaces across web, mobile, and other platforms.

b. The scope of the Project includes collaborative development under the Project License (as defined herein) supporting the mission, including documentation, testing, integration, and the creation of other artifacts that aid the development, deployment, operation, or adoption of the open source project.

c. The Project's technical governance is built on the principle that those closest to the work are best positioned to set its technical direction; that authority should be delegated to empowered working groups; and that governance itself should continuously improve.

### 2. Leadership Council

a. The Leadership Council (the "Council") will be responsible for all technical oversight of the open source Project. The Council serves as the central coordination body for the Project's technical governance and as decision-maker of last resort.

b. The Council is composed of representatives designated by each working group, with one representative per working group. No individual may serve as the representative of more than one working group simultaneously.

c. Responsibilities. The Council will be responsible for all aspects of oversight relating to the Project, which may include:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;i. coordinating the technical direction of the Project and establishing the official position of the Project as a whole;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ii. creating, modifying, dissolving, and reorganizing working groups;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iii. delegating decision-making authority to working groups and other structures as broadly as possible;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iv. establishing and maintaining policies that govern the operation of the Project, including the Working Group Policy, the Leadership Council Operations Policy, and the React Change Proposal Policy;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;v. coordinating cross-working-group efforts and resolving cross-cutting concerns;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;vi. determining the Project's technical representative to the React Foundation board;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;vii. establishing community norms, workflows, release policies, and security issue reporting policies; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;viii. coordinating with the series manager of the Project (as provided for in the Series Agreement, the "Series Manager") to resolve matters or concerns that may arise.

d. Operating constraints. The Council operates under the following constraints:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;i. Delegate work, do not perform it: the Council should not take on work beyond what this Charter explicitly assigns to it; work must be delegated to existing or new working groups wherever possible.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ii. Seek consensus before formal action: the Council should look for ways to use its powers as sparingly as possible, preferring consensus-building over voting.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iii. Act in good faith: representatives must make decisions in the best interest of the Project as a whole, even when those decisions conflict with their individual working groups, employers, or other outside interests.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iv. Be transparent: the Council should be as open and transparent about decision-making as practical.

e. The detailed procedures governing the Council's operation, including representative terms, alternates, vacancies, participation requirements, candidate criteria, designated communication spaces, and the selection of the React Foundation representative, are maintained in the Leadership Council Operations Policies. The Leadership Council Operations Policies may be amended by the Council through its public policy decision process without requiring an amendment to this Charter.

### 3. Decision-Making

a. Consent as default. The Council operates by consent-based decision-making. Consent means that no representative's stated requirements may be disregarded; all representatives must find a proposal acceptable enough to proceed, though they need not prefer it over alternatives.

b. Approval requirements. For a Council decision to take effect:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;i. the proposal must be posted in a designated Council communication space;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ii. the proposal must be reviewed and consented to by a quorum of Council representatives;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iii. there must be no outstanding explicit objections from any Council representative; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iv. a minimum feedback period must have elapsed before the decision takes effect.

The specific quorum threshold, the designated communication spaces, the minimum feedback period, and related procedural details are set forth in the Leadership Council Operations Policy.

c. Decisions requiring public proposal. Certain decisions must be publicly proposed and decided through a public process, including:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;i. any decision that changes this Charter, changes the Working Group Policy, or changes policies governing eligibility for Council representatives; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ii. any policy decision (as opposed to a one-off operational decision), including legal or licensing policy decisions, changes to the Code of Conduct, agreements making ongoing commitments to other projects or organizations on behalf of the Project, and decisions to retain a class of future decisions within the Council rather than delegating them.

d. Unresolved decisions. In the event a Council decision cannot be resolved, any Council representative may refer the matter to the Series Manager for assistance in reaching a resolution.

### 4. Working Groups

Working groups are the primary organizational units of the Project. Each working group has ultimate authority over decisions within its defined purview and designates one representative to the Leadership Council.

a. Authority. Working groups are the ultimate decision-makers within their purview. Working groups manage their own membership, internal structure, and operational processes, subject to the constraints established in this Charter and the Working Group Policy.

b. Establishment and modification. The Leadership Council establishes, modifies, dissolves, and reorganizes working groups through its public policy decision process. Removal of a working group requires the consent of all Council representatives except the representative of the working group being removed; that representative must be invited to deliberations concerning the removal, and removal over their objection should occur only in extreme cases.

c. Working Group Policy. The current roster of working groups, the criteria they must satisfy, and the procedures governing their creation, modification, membership, leadership, and operation are maintained in the Working Group Policy. The Working Group Policy may be amended by the Leadership Council through its public policy decision process without requiring an amendment to this Charter.

d. Representation and transparency. Each working group designates exactly one representative to the Council. A current list of members for each working group must be displayed publicly. Working groups must operate transparently, with technical discussion taking place on public forums to the extent practical.

### 5. React Foundation Relationship

a. The Project's technical governance under this Charter and the organizational governance of the React Foundation are intentionally separate and distinct. The React Foundation has no authority over the technical direction of the Project.

b. A representative selected by the Leadership Council from among its members serves as the primary bridge between the Project's technical governance and the React Foundation's organizational governance. The selection process and term of this representative are governed by the Leadership Council Operations Policy.

### 6. Change Proposals

a. Substantive technical changes to the Project follow the React Change Proposal (RCP) process, which establishes a staged lifecycle for evaluating, experimenting with, and adopting new features or significant modifications.

b. The RCP process is maintained in the React Change Proposal Policy. The React Change Proposal Policy may be amended by the Leadership Council through its public policy decision process without requiring an amendment to this Charter.

### 7. Compliance with Policies

a. This Charter is subject to the Series Agreement for the Project and the Operating Agreement of LF Projects. Contributors will comply with the policies of LF Projects as may be adopted and amended by LF Projects, including, without limitation, the policies listed at https://lfprojects.org/policies/.

b. The Council may adopt a code of conduct ("CoC") for the Project, which is subject to approval by the Series Manager. In the event that a Project-specific CoC has not been approved, the LF Projects Code of Conduct listed at https://lfprojects.org/policies will apply for all Collaborators in the Project.

c. When amending or adopting any policy applicable to the Project, LF Projects will publish such policy, as to be amended or adopted, on its web site at least 30 days prior to such policy taking effect; provided, however, that in the case of any amendment of the Trademark Policy or Terms of Use of LF Projects, any such amendment is effective upon publication on LF Project's web site.

d. All Collaborators must allow open participation from any individual or organization meeting the requirements for contributing under this Charter and any policies adopted for all Collaborators by the Council, regardless of competitive interests. The Project community must not seek to exclude any participant based on any criteria, requirement, or reason other than those that are reasonable and applied on a non-discriminatory basis to all Collaborators in the Project community.

e. The Project will operate in a transparent, open, collaborative, and ethical manner at all times. The output of all Project discussions, proposals, timelines, decisions, and status should be made open and easily visible to all. Any potential violations of this requirement should be reported immediately to the Series Manager.

### 8. Community Assets

a. LF Projects (or an associated hosting entity) will hold title to all trade or service marks used by the Project ("Project Trademarks"), whether based on common law or registered rights. Project Trademarks will be transferred and assigned to LF Projects (or, where applicable, the associated hosting entity) to hold on behalf of the Project. Any use of any Project Trademarks by Collaborators in the Project will (a) either be (i) in a way that constitutes fair use or (ii) in accordance with the license from LF Projects to the Project and the applicable trademark usage guidelines and (b) inure to the benefit of LF Projects (or the associated hosting entity).

b. The Project will, as permitted and in accordance with such license from LF Projects, develop and own all Project GitHub and social media accounts, and domain name registrations created by the Project community.

c. Under no circumstances will LF Projects be expected or required to undertake any action on behalf of the Project that is inconsistent with the tax-exempt status or purpose, as applicable, of the Joint Development Foundation or LF Projects, LLC.

### 9. General Rules and Operations

a. The Project will:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;i. engage in the work of the Project in a professional manner consistent with maintaining a cohesive community, while also maintaining the goodwill and esteem of LF Projects, Joint Development Foundation, and other partner organizations in the open source community; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ii. respect the rights of all trademark owners, including any branding and trademark usage guidelines.

### 10. Intellectual Property Policy

a. Collaborators acknowledge that the copyright in all new contributions will be retained by the copyright holder as independent works of authorship and that no contributor or copyright holder will be required to assign copyrights to the Project.

b. Except as described in Section 10.c., all contributions to the Project are subject to the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;i. All new inbound code contributions to the Project must be made using the MIT License (the "Project License").

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ii. All new inbound code contributions must also be accompanied by a Developer Certificate of Origin (http://developercertificate.org) sign-off in the source code system that is submitted through a Council-approved contribution process, which will bind the authorized contributor and, if not self-employed, their employer to the applicable license.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iii. All outbound code will be made available under the Project License.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iv. Documentation will be received and made available by the Project under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License (available at http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;v. The Project may seek to integrate and contribute back to other open source projects ("Upstream Projects"). In such cases, the Project will conform to all license requirements of the Upstream Projects, including dependencies, leveraged by the Project. Upstream Project code contributions not stored within the Project's main code repository will comply with the contribution process and license terms for the applicable Upstream Project.

c. The Council may approve the use of an alternative license or licenses for inbound or outbound contributions on an exception basis. To request an exception, the requester should describe the contribution, the alternative open source license(s), and the justification for using an alternative open source license for the Project. License exceptions must be approved by a two-thirds vote of the entire Council.

d. Contributed files should contain license information, such as SPDX short-form identifiers, indicating the open source license or licenses pertaining to the file.

### 11. Amendments

a. This Charter may be amended only through the following process:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;i. the proposed amendment must be posted publicly as a diff against the current text and remain open for public feedback for at least 30 days;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ii. the amendment must receive the unanimous consent of all Leadership Council representatives; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;iii. the amendment is subject to approval by LF Projects.

b. Exception: where an amendment would result in the removal of an existing working group, the consent of the representative of the working group being removed is not required, but that representative must be invited to deliberations concerning the removal, and removal over their objection should occur only in extreme cases.

c. Council policies referenced in this Charter — including the Working Group Policy, the Leadership Council Operations Policy, and the React Change Proposal Policy — may be amended by the Council through the public policy decision process described in Section 3, without requiring an amendment to this Charter.
